Specification, assumptions and interpretation

The empirical core is a separate VAR for Germany and each non-overlapping A*10 sector. Variables are quarterly log changes in real gross value added, employment, real compensation per employee and the gross-value-added deflator. AIC selects 1–4 lags on training data; expanding-window one-quarter forecasts evaluate the last 12 quarters against a historical-mean-growth benchmark. The final model is refitted on the full sample.

Generalized impulses use the residual covariance matrix, normalized to a one-percentage-point innovation in the selected growth variable. Their cumulative log responses are converted to percentage level deviations. Ordering is not used to assert causal identification. Pandemic observations remain in the sample; structural breaks and short sector histories can affect estimates.

The optional neoclassical-synthesis closure keeps the VAR output path, partially adjusts prices and nominal wages, and links labour to Y = A Kα L1−α with fixed productivity. Its capital share is a clipped recent non-compensation share of nominal value added, not a structural elasticity estimate. Investment responds to output through the chosen accelerator. Annual net capital stocks imply no observed quarterly capital path; simulated capital uses a calibrated quarterly investment/capital ratio of 0.015 and depreciation of 0.0125. All closure parameters are illustrative. This is an estimated reduced-form core with a calibrated closure, not an estimated DSGE.

The nominal exchange rate is fixed by scenario assumption, including against non-euro currencies. There is no domestic exchange-rate policy or endogenous ECB response. Real appreciation equals the domestic price response when foreign prices are fixed. Euro membership only fixes intra-euro nominal exchange rates in reality.

Output and employment are seasonally and calendar adjusted; compensation is seasonally adjusted. Real pay is compensation per employee deflated by the sector value-added deflator, not take-home pay or CPI purchasing power. Capital is annual net fixed assets in chain-linked 2020 million euros. Sector chain-linked output is not added up.

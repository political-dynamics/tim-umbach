03 Transparent by construction

A model you can
argue with.

The page separates three questions: expected vote share, the probability that a party combination clears 50% of modeled seats, and the probability that such a combination actually forms a government.

Poll-only logit

Up to twelve recent DAWUM polls are weighted by recency and square-root sample size. Party support is simulated on the log-odds scale, keeping predicted shares inside plausible bounds.

Economy-enriched probit

The second specification adds a capped economic-vote term: 0.32 × real-GDP growth − 0.55 × the change in the BA unemployment rate. A latent-normal simulation translates that signal into vote and seat uncertainty.

What “fit” means here

Backtests freeze the polling window before nine federal and state elections and report mean absolute party-vote error. They are a compact diagnostic, not enough evidence to claim structural forecasting superiority.

Important limits

Candidate names are Brave-verified where a matching current source is found and otherwise remain explicitly curated fallbacks. Their probabilities are inferred from modeled coalition paths, not direct candidate polling. Formation priors encode present political compatibility and can change. Tactical voting, direct mandates, turnout shocks, campaign effects, and the full state-specific seat laws are simplified.

04 / Sources

Data with a
paper trail.

Polling is derived from DAWUM’s ODbL-licensed open database. Economic inputs come from the German government, Destatis, and the Federal Employment Agency. Historical outcomes are checked against official election results.

This is a methodological portfolio project, not voting advice or a published election forecast.
